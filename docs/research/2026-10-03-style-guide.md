# Blocky Story Shorts: Mainstream Style Guide (20–30 s finished length)

Prepared 2026-10-03 for Blow Studios (cast: Bloxy, Pip). I took patterns only from the research below. I did not copy any creator's plot, dialogue, characters, catchphrases, thumbnails or branding. Every example line here is new.

## 1. Hooks (first 1–2 seconds)

**Rules**
1. The first spoken word lands by **0.3 s**. Never open on silence or an establishing wide shot.
2. Start **in the middle of the conflict**. The hook *is* the inciting incident, not the setup before it.
3. Fit the conflict into **7 words or fewer** (about 2 s at the engine's `fast` 3.3 words/s). Pair it with on-screen hook text of 6 words or fewer at the top of the frame for 0–2.5 s.
4. Within 2 s the viewer should know three things: **who** is wronged or wants something, **what** is at stake, and **why it is unfair, strange or urgent**.
5. Open on a close-up with motion (push-in, a prop being shoved at camera, a character mid-jump).
6. The hook makes a promise. The payoff must keep it, or viewers bounce and dislike.

**Hook formulas**

| # | Formula | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| A | Accusation | "[He/She/Rook] just [did unfair thing]." |
| B | Forbidden instruction | "Don't [action]…" and the other character does it at 1.2 s |
| C | Status flip | "[They] laughed at my [humble thing]." |
| D | Countdown | "I have [N] seconds before [consequence]." |
| E | POV | "POV: you're the only one who [noticed X]." |
| F | Choice | "Pick one: [A] or [B]?" |
| G | Secret | "Nobody knows [character] is secretly [X]." |
| H | Impact-first | Open on the fall, crash or gasp SFX, then the line explains it |
| I | Betrayal-or-misunderstanding | "[Friend] has been lying to me for [time]." The lie turns out kind. |
| J | Series recap | "Part 2: [stake restated in 5 words]." |

## 2. Title and caption formulas

**Title rules:** 30–55 characters. Put the conflict in the first 3 words. Use at most one ellipsis and 1–2 emoji that match the real emotion. The title must be true to the video. Put hashtags at the end of the description, not in the title, when the title is long.

| Formula | Original example |
|---|---|
| Pronoun + unfair act + emoji | "He Laughed at Her Tiny Obby 😳" |
| First person + twist promise | "I Opened the Chest… Instant Regret 😭" |
| Status flip | "Noob to Legend in One Jump 🤯" |
| Choice question | "Which Key Would YOU Pick? 🔑" |
| POV | "POV: Your Little Sister Is an Obby Pro" |
| Secret | "Our Teacher Has a Secret… 🤫" |
| Emotional (true) | "Nobody Came to Dot's Party… 🥺" |
| Series | "The Tiny Obby (Part 2)" |

**Retitles for the current batch** (same stories):
- The Golden Coin Trade → "He Said This Coin Was RARE… 💀"
- The Lava Dare → "He Dared Me to Touch the Lava 😳"
- The Perfect Speedrun → "My Best Run EVER… Nobody Timed It 💀"
- The Acceptance Speech → "I Practised My Winner Speech… 🥺"
- Homework Panic → "I Forgot My Homework… Or Did I? 😰"

**Description or caption (YouTube and TikTok):**
1. Line 1 restates the hook as a question that invites a comment: "Would you forgive Rook? 👇"
2. Line 2 discloses honestly: "Original animated story with blocky characters."
3. Add 3–5 hashtags: `#shorts #animation #story #obby` plus one topic tag (`#siblings`, `#school`).
4. Pin a comment that asks the same question, or links Part 2.

A note on comments: several high-view items showed almost no comments (6 comments on 2.36 M views; 1 comment on 632 K). That is consistent with comments being off, possibly because the videos are set as made for kids. If the channel's audience setting turns comments off, choice and "comment below" endings lose their purpose. The owner should decide the audience setting first.

## 3. Story structures for 20–30 s, with beat timings

**General timing rules (25 s plan):** the hook is at 0–2 s. State the stakes by **5 s**. Put the first turn at **55–65 %** of the runtime and the final twist or payoff at **80–90 %**. End **within 1 s** of the punchline. Ideally the last frame or line flows back into the first, so the video loops and gets rewatched.

### T1. Karma flip (most versatile), 25 s
| t (s) | Beat | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Hook | The unfair act, shown and said (rival mocks or cheats) |
| 2–5 | Stakes | Underdog answers in one line; the witness reacts ("Ooh.") |
| 5–11 | Escalation ×2 | Rival doubles down; quick 1 s action cuts |
| 11–14 | Turn | A reveal flips status (hidden skill, a rule, the real scale of the challenge) |
| 14–19 | Reaction | Rival realises; music drops out; one-word reactions |
| 19–23 | Payoff | Harmless comic backfire **plus a moment of decency** (apology, help) |
| 23–25 | Button | A callback line or "Part 2?" text; loop point |

### T2. Secret or mystery box, 20–24 s
Hook question (0–2) → three quick wrong guesses at about 1.5 s each (2–7) → stakes ("If it's X, I lose…", 7–10) → reveal (10–14) → **double twist** that recontextualises the reveal (14–19) → button (19–22).

### T3. Choice or "would you rather", 20–25 s
Each option is about 5 s, made of a premise, a value and a catch, with the catches escalating: 0–2 premise ("Pick one…"), 2–7 option 1, 7–12 option 2, 12–18 option 3, then the cast argues for 2 s and the question goes to camera ("Which one? 👇"). This format had by far the highest comment and like rates in the sample. Never promise a fake unlock or reward for liking or subscribing.

### T4. Sad turning warm (the biggest emotional lane), 30 s
0–2 a lonely or hurt moment shown plainly ("Nobody came.") → 2–8 why it matters → 8–15 a second blow (sad music) → 15–20 a small act of kindness from an unexpected character → 20–26 the reason is revealed (they were planning a surprise, or the rival remembered) → 26–30 a warm button. **No death, illness, real tragedies or "true story" claims.** The sadness should come from friendship, missing out or a lost object, and it must resolve.

### T5. Part 1 cliffhanger, 30 s
Hook to escalation as in T1. At **26–28 s**, cut on the peak decision (a door opens, a choice is about to be made). Text: "Part 2?" Part 1 must still pay off one mini-promise, so it is not pure bait. Part 2 opens with a **2 s recap line**, posts within 24–48 h, and is linked in a pinned comment. Use cliffhangers for **at most about 1 in 4** videos. Five of the 44 outliers carried part or series numbers, so the device works but should not dominate.

**Ending mix per 12-video batch:** 4 karma or comic twists, 3 warm endings, 2 double twists or callbacks, 2 choices or questions, 1 Part 1 cliffhanger.

## 4. Dialogue style

**Measured:**
- Mainstream transcripts ran at 4.4 words/s (an 86 s narrated school story), 3.5 words/s (a 26 s choice video) and 5.5 words/s (a 22 s voice-over).
- Quoted character lines inside the narrated story averaged **5 words** (range 1–10). Narration sentences averaged about 11 words.
- The current batch runs at 1.1 words/s, with 4.1 words per line.

**Targets:**
- **55–85 spoken words per 25 s** (about 2.2–3.4 words/s overall), leaving holes for SFX and reaction beats. Use `fast` pace for setup and banter and `slow` only for the reveal line.
- **12–18 lines** per 25–30 s, one every 1.5–2.5 s. Speakers alternate every 1–2 lines.
- **Line length:** 2–8 words, 12 at most, one idea per line. One-word reaction lines ("Wait.", "Bro.", "No way.") count toward rhythm.
- **State the stakes explicitly** in one line by 5 s: "If I fall, Rook keeps my badge."
- **Narration option:** the protagonist narrates in first person and past tense between in-scene lines: "I had one try. Rook was watching." If the plan format cannot hold an off-screen narrator, have the character say the line to camera (`eye_target: camera`). Viewers read that as POV.
- **Callback endings:** the last line repeats an earlier line with a new meaning.
- **Each character sounds different:** Bloxy is loud, short and impulsive. Pip is dry, a half-beat late, and understates. The new characters are covered in section 7.

**Slang that stays family-friendly**
- Use: "bro", "no way", "wait, WHAT", "let's gooo", "GG", "that's an L / that's a W", "lowkey", "sus" (playful), "noob" (only self-mocking or affectionate), "oof", "respawn", "built different", "not the [X]!"
- Avoid:
  - insults ("stupid", "loser", "idiot"); use "goofball" or "silly" instead
  - jokes about bodies or appearance
  - swearing, or bleeps that imply swearing
  - "kill/die/dead" in dialogue (use "eliminated", "respawn", "oof")
  - romance or dating talk
  - fast-dating meme slang (it ages within weeks)
  - lines that tell kids to share accounts, passwords or real money

## 5. Sound effects and music

- **SFX density:** 10–16 per 25–30 s, about one every 2 s. Put a `whoosh` on every whip or time jump, `pop` or `ding` on realisations, `boing` or `thud` on physical comedy, and `gasp_sting` on the reveal. `coin` and `sparkle` mark value moments; `drumroll` builds to a verdict.
- **`fail_horn`** at most once per five videos. It is the batch's crutch right now (7 of 12).
- **Music:** change the cue at every act turn (3–4 cues per video). Common arcs: `playful → tension → none (0.3–0.6 s silence) → triumph`, or `sad → sad → chill (warm)`. The **silence before the punchline** is the cheapest laugh amplifier there is.
- **Duck the music** under every line. Burned-in captions should follow the words exactly.
- **Rights:** use only the studio's own music and SFX library. Do not use recognisable meme-sound samples or chart songs.

## 6. Common tropes: saturation and how to keep them kind

| Trope | Seen in sample | Saturation | Kind, safe version | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sad / tear-jerker | About 11 of 44 outlier titles | Very high | Loneliness or missing out that resolves warmly; a lost object found | Death, illness, abandoned animals, real tragedies, "based on a true story" |
| Rich vs poor / noob vs pro / status flip | Billionaire↔homeless, old vs new comparisons | High | The underdog wins through skill or kindness; the rich rival learns and stays a friend | Mocking poverty or homelessness as a punchline |
| Bully gets karma | "They laughed at…", "…big mistake" | High | A harmless comic backfire (slips into a foam pit, loses the race), then an apology or redemption | Violence, humiliation that lingers, revenge plans kids could copy |
| Betrayal / scam / stealing | Several outliers by one channel; "best friend lying" | High | It is a misunderstanding (the "lie" was a surprise), **or** a safety lesson: too-good-to-be-true trades fail | Step-by-step scams, real-money or currency giveaways, account or password sharing, hacking |
| Crush / love / relationship | About 10 of 25 recent search titles, mostly low views | Saturated, often mature | **Skip.** Replace with friendship, admiration or "new kid wants to belong" | Dating, cheating, jealousy plots, anything in a bedroom context |
| Family / siblings ("me vs bro") | Older brother, sibling rivalry | Medium | Sibling rivalry with a loyal turn; grandparents and parents as secret legends | Parents depicted as cruel or neglectful |
| School | Cheating on tests, new kid | Medium | Panic, a fair teacher, the new kid; anyone who cheats always loses | Glorifying cheating |
| Obby / dare / challenge | Common game gags | Medium | Clearly in-game rules (respawn, checkpoints) | Dares that copy real-world danger (heat, heights, electricity) |
| Secret identity | "Discovers his secret" | Medium | The teacher was a champion; the little sister is a master builder | "Secret admin powers", exploits |
| Choice / would-you-rather | 4 of 44, with the highest comment and like rates | Low to medium (opportunity) | Fun, low-stakes options with honest catches | Fake "option unlocks if you subscribe" promises |
| POV | Common framing | Ubiquitous | Use as framing, not as the whole idea | — |
| Intruder / break-in | One outlier | — | Avoid, or make the "intruder" a sleepwalking friend (as we already did) | Home-invasion fear |

**Overdone wording to avoid:** "99 % can't predict", "made everyone cry", "based on true story", "big mistake!" in every title, "ranking top 3" compilations (these also tend to reuse other people's footage).

## 7. Cast: size, archetypes and three new recurring characters

**Typical size:** 2–3 speaking characters plus at most one silent extra. More than 4 does not read in 25 s. Archetypes that recur:
- the protagonist and viewer stand-in, often the underdog
- the rival or antagonist (show-off, cheater, "scammer")
- the witness or sidekick, who carries the reactions and the jokes
- the authority or judge (teacher, parent, referee)
- a family member (sibling, parent, grandparent)

The current cast has only protagonist and sidekick. **No one can be wrong, so there is no karma and no stakes.**

Costume note: the rig currently supports palette slots (skin, top, top_trim, pants, shoes, hair, eyes, brows, mouth, badge, hat), tops (hoodie, tee), hair (messy_block, short_block), a cap hat and a star badge. The ideas below work with palette alone. Items marked (new mesh) are optional rig additions.

### 1. Rook, the show-off rival
- **Role:** Rich-kid or braggart antagonist for karma and status-flip stories. Vain and competitive, but never cruel. Secretly insecure, and redeemable every time (apologises, helps by the end), so the audience can laugh at him without hating him.
- **Voice:** Announces himself in third person ("Rook doesn't do tutorials."), drawls slightly, counts his coins aloud.
- **Look:**
  - top: gold tee `#E3B23C`, trim `#FFFFFF`
  - pants: black `#1B1B2F`; shoes: white `#F4F1DE`
  - badge: diamond blue `#5BC0EB`
  - hair: short_block `#111111`; hat: cap in gold `#E3B23C` worn backwards (crown, new mesh)
  - default expression smug, posture proud, scale 1.05

### 2. Dot, Pip's little sister
- **Role:** Underdog, family and secret-identity character. Tiny, earnest and blunt. A genius obby builder and speedrunner whom everyone underestimates. She adds sibling stories, "the new kid" stories and big reveals.
- **Voice:** Short bursts, too honest ("Your jump was bad."), excited squeaks.
- **Look:**
  - scale **0.72** (instantly readable as younger)

### 3. Ms. Tally, teacher, referee and fair judge
- **Role:** Authority figure who hands out the karma verdict, and who also works as a shopkeeper or obby referee. Deadpan and fair. Running secret: she is a retired legendary obby champion, which gives an easy "the teacher can do it" reveal.
- **Voice:** Rarely speaks; every line is a verdict ("Pencils down." "Rule one: be kind.").
- **Look:**
  - scale 1.12
  - top: navy tee `#2E4057` (cardigan, new mesh), trim mustard `#E1A140`

## 8. Camera and cut rhythm

This section was not measured: no video frames were analysed. Treat it as practice to A/B test against your analytics.

- **Average shot length 1.2–1.8 s**, which is **14–22 shots per 25–30 s** (about 0.6–0.8 cuts/s), against the current 8 shots of 3.75 s.
- **First shot 1.5 s or shorter**: a close-up or medium close-up already in motion (push_in). No establishing wide in the first 2 s. Use a 0.8 s wide later only when geography matters.
- **Cut on every change of speaker.** On lines longer than 2 s, punch in mid-line (medium → close_up, same side).
- **Escalation montage:** 0.6–1.0 s shots (hops, coins, the clock), each with an SFX.
- **The reveal is the only long hold:** 2–2.5 s on the reaction close-up, after 0.3–0.6 s of silence.
- **Inserts:** 0.6–1.0 s close-ups of the key prop (sign, key, coin) so the twist reads without sound.
- **Angle grammar:**
  - `low` on the rival when he is winning and `high` on the underdog when down; swap them at the turn
  - `crane_up` for scale reveals and `whip` transitions for time jumps
  - `extreme_close_up` on the eyes for a 0.5 s shock gag
- **Cut away to listeners:** 30–40 % of lines should play over the listener's reaction shot.
- **Captions** sit in the lower-middle safe zone; the hook text sits in the top band (0–2.5 s). Keep both clear of the platform UI zones.
- Check the validator and QA for any minimum shot length, and confirm `whip` timing before moving to 1 s shots.

## 10. What to avoid

- **Misleading claims:** fake statistics ("99 % can't…"), "true story" or "made players cry" claims, fake unlocks or giveaways, titles the video does not deliver, presenting animation as real gameplay.
- **Real brands and platforms:** no real game or experience names, logos, UI or currency in titles, thumbnails or story. The studio renders original blocky characters in Blender, so using the "Roblox" name, logo or its experience names in titles, tags or thumbnails risks trademark and misleading-metadata problems. Current plans tag `roblox`. This is the owner's decision after checking the brand's published guidelines and YouTube's metadata policy; "blocky", "obby" and "animation" are safer.
- **Real people:** no YouTubers, celebrities, real events or real tragedies. One outlier in the sample retold a real child's photo over game-style footage, and that is exactly the pattern to avoid.
- **Unsafe behaviour:** dangerous real-world dares, sharing passwords or accounts, meeting strangers, scams presented as how-tos, hacking or exploits, violence, romance or dating content, death or abandonment.
- **Copying:** never reuse a creator's plot sequence or twist mechanics, character names, catchphrases, title series, thumbnail layout, or music or voice style. Run every plan through the studio originality screen. Keep `inspiration.note` honest.
- **Platform settings:** synthetic voices on cartoon characters generally do not need a realistic-content AI disclosure, but confirm the current YouTube rule. Set the audience (made for kids or not) deliberately, because it changes comments, and therefore which endings work.

## 11. Quick QA checklist (score 1 point each; aim for 9 or more of 12)

1. A line is spoken by 0.3 s, and the hook text is 6 words or fewer.
2. The conflict is clear by 2 s; the stakes are stated by 5 s.
3. The title is a conflict or promise sentence, true to the video, with 1–2 fitting emoji.
4. 55–85 words and 12–18 lines; no line over 12 words.
5. 14–22 shots, and no shot over 3 s except the reveal hold.
6. 10–16 SFX, at least 3 music cues, and one silence before the punchline.
7. There is a rival, judge or family member, not only the two friends.
8. The twist raises stakes or flips status; it is not an "it was nothing" deflation.
9. The ending fits the batch mix (karma, warm, double twist, choice, cliffhanger).
10. The last line or frame invites a rewatch or a comment.
11. Kind: whoever is wronged gets justice, and whoever does wrong gets a harmless backfire plus a decent moment.
12. No real brands, people or events, no unsafe behaviour, and no copied structure.

- Title counts show what is common, not why videos performed.
- No frames or thumbnails were analysed, so the cut-rhythm and visual advice in section 8 is practice to test, not a measurement.
- The like and comment rates are a single snapshot. Comments may be off on made-for-kids videos.
- Treat every recommendation as a hypothesis to A/B test against the channel's own retention data.
